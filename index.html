About

I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Science at the University of Otago, working on AI for clinical decision-making. My research asks how language models can read clinical records reliably, and how they can act more like clinicians: gathering evidence step by step before reaching a diagnosis.

My current work covers automated ICD coding for rare, long-tail diagnoses, Semantic IDs for medical coding, and medical AI agents for interactive diagnosis. Before the PhD I trained in data science at the University of Leeds and worked as a data analyst in industry.

Research

Long-tail automated medical coding Published

Most ICD codes are rare in clinical data, making them difficult for models to learn and easy to overlook. CoLa-ICD addresses this long-tail problem by enriching code representations with medical knowledge—including UMLS synonyms and LLM-generated clinical terms—and modeling relationships between codes through a co-occurrence graph. It then uses term-level attention and label-aware contrastive learning to better align clinical evidence with the appropriate ICD codes. We evaluate the approach on both MIMIC-III (ICD-9) and MIMIC-IV (ICD-10).

Medical AI agents for interactive diagnosis Ongoing

Diagnosis rarely happens in a single step. Clinicians gather evidence over multiple interactions, continually refining a differential diagnosis as new information becomes available. I'm developing a language-model agent that learns this iterative process: deciding what to ask next, updating its diagnostic hypotheses, and eventually producing a final diagnosis. The project combines knowledge-guided diagnostic trajectories with reinforcement learning to improve both diagnostic reasoning and interaction efficiency.

Semantic IDs for medical coding Ongoing

ICD codes are symbolic identifiers whose surface forms carry little information about the clinical concepts they represent. I'm exploring Semantic IDs that reorganize the label space so that clinically related diagnoses share parts of a structured token sequence. Rather than generating ICD codes directly, language models predict these semantic representations under constrained decoding and map them back to valid ICD codes. The goal is to make the output space easier to learn by exposing relationships between labels that are hidden in conventional ICD identifiers.
